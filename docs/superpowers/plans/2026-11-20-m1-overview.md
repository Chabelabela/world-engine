# M1 Spatial Engine — Overview Plan

**Milestone goal (ROADMAP §5):** break Minecraft's flat-world assumption. A player can walk or fly around the home planet's equator and over a pole and come back to where they started. Terrain never visibly breaks at face seams, and the sun rises and sets with the planet's rotation.

**Why there are several plans:** M1 mixes pure math (verifiable now), an experiment whose outcome shapes the Minecraft-side design, and Minecraft-facing code. Following writing-plans' scope rule, each phase gets its own plan that produces working, testable software. M1e and M1f (seams, sky) are written **after** the spike's design record (ADR), so they build on evidence rather than guesses. M1d (world preset, layout, chunk generator) is written before the ADR because none of it touches ships or handoffs. Its one ADR-sensitive choice, which level each planet lives in, is kept as data in `PlanetSpec`.

## Design decisions already made (in M1a, verified by tests)

1. **Face orientation.** Each face's local axes (east, up, south) are a *proper rotation* of Minecraft's (X, Y, Z). Blocks crossing a seam rotate by quarter turns and are never mirrored, so stairs, doors and other facing blocks stay valid.
2. **Equal-angle projection, radius 2N/π.** One block spans exactly 1 m along the equator. For N = 16,384 that gives R = 10,430.38 m. Measured distortion: cell area 0.707–1.0 of the centre cell, aspect ratio ≤ √2, and grid lines meeting at 60–120°. These are the theoretical limits of equal-angle.
3. **Seams fold the unfolded cube, not the sphere.** Crossing an edge continues on the neighbouring face as if the cube's surface were laid flat across that edge. Whole cells map to whole cells, so blocks line up one-to-one across seams. On the sphere, the first block past a seam is 0.82–1.0 m from the last block before it. A wrong mapping, such as a mirrored edge, would be 12.8 km away, and the tests catch it. Grid lines **kink** at seams everywhere except an edge's midpoint (up to about 60° near corners). So `QuarterTurns` is exact for block facing, yaw and grid velocity, while physical velocities cross through the body frame. M1e must respect this.
4. **The level layout is a cross-shaped net.** E0 | E1 | E2 | E3 sit in a row, with North above E1 and South below it. Five of the cube's 12 edges are ordinary adjacency in the level. The equator wraps from E3 back to E0 by a pure translation (no rotation). So walking all the way around the equator crosses exactly one seam.
5. **Frames:**
   - *Surface frame:* a face's block grid.
   - *Body frame:* planet-centred, spinning with the planet.
   - *Inertial frame:* planet-centred, not spinning.
   - The surface → inertial → surface round trip is accurate to about 10⁻¹² m and 10⁻¹⁴ m/s, against the roadmap's budget of 1 cm and 1 mm/s.
6. **Time precision.** The M0 review suggested reducing `t − epoch` by the orbital period. Analysis showed that this can't beat the precision of `t` itself (an f64 second count). At 10⁹ s (about 30 years of game time), the error is about 0.03 mm either way. So it was dropped. The universe clock's representation is decided in M3 (integer ticks are the likely answer).

## What the spike (M1b) must answer before M1d–M1f are planned

**Answered:** see [ADR 0001](../../adr/0001-frames-and-handoff.md), "Evidence". Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 and Q5 are met. Q1b (pop-in after a teleport) is not met as asked, and the design avoids depending on it.

- **Q1 Teleport within one level:** is a same-level teleport between a planet face and a shipyard plot free of any loading screen? How many frames of missing chunks does it cause, with and without pre-loading the destination?
- **Q2 Ship rendering:** can blocks stored in a shipyard plot be rendered at an arbitrary transform through Minecraft 26.3's GPU abstraction, with Sodium installed, cheaply enough?
- **Q3 Ship sync:** should the client get ship blocks through vanilla chunk tracking (extended by a mixin) or through a custom ship-snapshot payload?
- **Q4 The handoff:** can a player inside a moving ship move from the surface frame to the ship frame and back with at most one visible pop? If not, what does the masking fallback look like?
- **Q5 Multiplayer:** are two clients aboard one ship consistent through a handoff, and how much bandwidth does it cost?

The answers go into `docs/adr/0001-frames-and-handoff.md`, together with the list of mixins the design needs.

## Inputs for the M1d plan (from the M1c reviews)

- **Level height:** terrain heights are guaranteed within `we_planet::MIN_HEIGHT..=MAX_HEIGHT` (−400..700 m above sea level). Size the universe level's `min_y` and height from these, plus room for builds and the atmosphere.
- **Planet definitions:** add a `PlanetSpec` (seed, cells, kind, layout origin, sea-level block y) per planet, as the single source for `HOME_CELLS` and friends. Today these are constants repeated in Java and tests.
- **Chunks vs. faces:** `we_terrain_columns` rejects any region that crosses a face edge. Align face origins in the level to multiples of 16, so every chunk lies on exactly one face (16,384 and 4,096 are multiples of 16). Chunks in seam bands and void gaps are not generated from terrain.
- **Failure policy:** decide what a worldgen thread does if a native call throws: log once and fill the chunk with a safe fallback, or crash with a clear message. Never leave a half-written chunk.
- **World format:** store `TERRAIN_VERSION` (and the ABI version) in the world's saved data, so a later terrain change is detected instead of silently mixing old and new chunks.
- **Debug commands:** gate `/worldengine …` behind an operator permission level using 26.3's permission API.
- **Sea-level fill:** heights are `floor(h)` relative to the datum, and `h < 0` is water. Define once where water fills to (datum or datum − 1 in block y) and where the datum sits in the level.
- **Biome resolution:** Minecraft stores biomes on a 4×4×4 grid. Decide whether to sample biomes per quart (16× fewer columns) or reuse the 16×16 chunk call (0.23 ms, likely fine). Map the 13 `PlanetBiome`s to Minecraft biomes in one table.
- **CI:** cargo steps run with `--locked`, so lockfile drift fails the build.

## Verification environment

- **Rust:** verified in any session, including cloud sessions. Run `cargo test`, `clippy` and `fmt`. Property tests use `proptest`.
- **Java and Minecraft:** need JDK 25, which is unavailable in the cloud planning environment. So Java changes are verified by **CI on GitHub Actions** (Linux, Windows and macOS). The pure bridge logic (`nativebridge/*`, no Minecraft classes) can also be compiled and exercised locally on JDK 21 with `--enable-preview`. The one source change needed is replacing Java 25's `allocate(layout, count)` with `allocateArray`. In-game behavior is checked by hand and, from M1e on, by Fabric GameTests in CI.

## Risks specific to M1

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Seam bands collide near the 8 cube corners.** Two bands claim the same W×W square of the level. | M1e makes those squares no-man's land (barrier or deep ocean). M1c biases terrain to deep ocean near corners, as the roadmap already plans. |
| **Chunk generation runs on worker threads.** | M1c's FFI is stateless and thread-safe by design: no `Universe` handle, and the planet description is passed in. |
| **Worldgen API churn in 26.x** (world presets, chunk generator codecs) | **Handled in the M1d plan:** every signature was read from the 26.3 jar, and the plan's code was built and GameTested in CI before the plan was written. 26.3 did change a lot (see the plan's "26.3 API notes"). |
| **Cross-platform float determinism.** `tan` and `atan` (used by `CubeSphere`) come from each platform's libm and can differ in the last bit between Linux, Windows and macOS. Seed-keyed terrain must be bit-identical on client and server. | M1c samples terrain through a deterministic math path (the `libm` crate or fixed polynomials), and adds a golden-hash test that runs on all three CI operating systems before any seed-keyed terrain ships. **Done in M1c:** a `libm` projection, hash-only noise, and golden hashes that pass on Linux x64/arm64, Windows and macOS. `glam` and `libm` are pinned to exact versions. |
| **`we-frames` becomes an ABI once Java depends on it** | M1c, before adding the `we-frames` FFI: build `PlanetLayout` from a `CubeSphere` (a single source of `cells`); validate inputs at the FFI boundary instead of reaching debug-assert paths (`cell_of`); and treat the `Face` numbering 0–5 as part of `WE_ABI_VERSION`. |
| **Vulkan transition affects the spike's rendering experiments** | The spike renders only through Minecraft's GPU abstraction, and tests on 26.3 stable plus one current 26.4 snapshot if feasible. |
